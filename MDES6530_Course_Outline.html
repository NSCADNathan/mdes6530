// Course Thesis

This studio explores the frameworks an interaction hangs off — how a designed layer of interaction gets built between an object and a user. Approached through a design lens: the work isn't a critical statement the way a piece of pure art would be, it has to function as an interaction. Students get at that framework a few different ways across the semester — discovery and UX/interaction analysis of existing designed objects, a close case study of an industry's design culture, and a focused unit on constraint as one generative design method among several. Students leave with a working interactive prototype and a design framework they can defend.

// Workload

18 hrs/week minimum expected per student. In-class time tops out at 7 hrs/session (one day/week) — the remaining 11 hrs/week is private studio time outside class. Theory content is capped at 1–2 hrs per session; every session mixes skills content with studio time.

No class Sept 7–11 (mandatory Collaborative Design Challenge, all grad students).

// Learning Objectives

By the end of this studio, students will be able to:

  • Investigate designed objects through technological archaeology — systematic dissection and cultural/UX analysis of found electro-mechanical devices
  • Apply constraint as a generative design method, and analyze a design culture from both a generative and critical angle
  • Design for accessibility broadly construed — physical/sensory disability, neurodivergence, and technophobia as legitimate, distinct entry points
  • Build technical fluency in physical computing — Arduino/ESP32 programming, fabrication, and AI-assisted tools, understood critically as well as practically
  • Structure and deploy a project professionally, including a companion app extending hardware interactivity beyond a browser interface
  • Design and build a working interactive prototype under a self-authored design framework they can defend

// Assessment Structure

Weekly Self-Assessment & Reflection — 40%

Consistent engagement and growth analysis across the semester — a running reflective record that tracks the evolution of each student's design framework session to session, feeding directly into the final crit defense.

Technological Archaeology Project — 15%

Students perform forensic dissection of an electro-mechanical consumer device (1950s–1990s), documenting its construction, componentry, and cultural role.

Component Weighting:
  • Investigation Manifesto: 25%
  • Component Dossier: 10%
  • Reverse-Engineered Schematics: 10%
  • Dissection Chronicle: 10%
  • Behavioral Analysis: 10%
  • Cultural Reflection: 35%

Hand Made Circuits — 15%

Students build a working circuit using non-manufactured materials — copper tape, conductive ink, graphite pencil, kitchen foil, salt water, found metal — refusing the standard PCB.

Component Weighting:
  • Artist Statement: 40%
  • Material Catalog: 10%
  • Circuit Diagram: 10%
  • Process Documentation: 10%
  • Functional Description: 10%
  • Critical Reflection: 20%

Accessibility Object — 15%

Students design and build a custom input device addressing one of the accessibility entry points from Session 9 — physical/sensory disability, neurodivergence, or technophobia — as a generative design constraint.

Component Weighting:
  • Interaction Manifesto: 40%
  • Technical Specifications: 10%
  • Circuit Architecture: 10%
  • Construction Documentation: 10%
  • Code Logic: 10%
  • User Experience Analysis: 20%

Final Interactive Prototype — 15%

Students design and build a working interactive prototype under a self-authored design framework, developed from the Session 5 proposal through the Session 10 mid-point and to the Dec 10 final crits.

Project Requirements:
  • Arduino/ESP32-based interactive system
  • Functioning input and output layer
  • Demonstrable interactivity between object and user
  • Defensible design framework, written and oral

Grading Philosophy

Self-assessment with instructor review. Students assess their own learning and assign a grade; the instructor will not lower self-assigned grades but reserves the right to grade higher when students undervalue their work. This applies across every graded component above.

// Teaching Methodology

This studio employs experiential learning methodologies prioritizing hands-on engagement with physical materials and systems. Key pedagogical approaches include:

  • Visceral Introduction to Concepts: Abstract principles are made concrete through direct physical experience before theoretical explanation
  • Reverse Engineering / Dissection: Students learn by analyzing and understanding existing systems before creating new ones
  • Progressive Complexity: Concepts build incrementally from simple to complex, ensuring a solid foundation
  • Safe Failure Environment: Students are encouraged to experiment, break components, and learn from failures
  • Self-Directed Assessment: Students develop metacognitive skills by assessing their own learning
